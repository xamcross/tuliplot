# The best start pages in 2026

"Best browser start page" searches turn up lists that all do the same thing: rank five or six tools by opinion and call it done. That's not very useful, because these tools aren't actually competing for the same job. Some save tabs you'll reopen later. Some group tabs into projects you save and restore. Some build a page out of bookmarks and widgets. One renders the actual sites live, all at once, in a fixed grid.

So instead of a ranked list, here's a list organized by what you're actually trying to do, with real prices and real limits for each tool, not marketing copy. One of the entries below is ours. We'll say so plainly when we get there, and it isn't first.

## Start with what your browser already gives you

Before installing anything, know what's already free. Every major browser lets you set a start page, a home page, or a new tab page in its own settings, no extension required, and pinning a tab or bookmarking a handful of sites covers plenty of people's actual routine. If your day genuinely comes down to two or three sites, that built-in setup is the right answer, and everything below is solving a problem you may not have. [What is a browser start page](/blog/what-is-a-browser-start-page) covers the differences between those three browser settings in more detail if you want it.

## If you save more tabs than you use: Toby

Toby's own pitch is "Stop Drowning in Tabs," and that's an honest description of the job: you save a tab's link into a Toby collection, close the tab, and reopen the saved link whenever you actually need it again. It's a tool for people whose browser fills up with research links, articles, and reference tabs faster than they can use them.

The free "Starter" plan costs nothing and lets you save and manage up to 60 tabs across all your collections, with no cap on how many members can join the account. Past that limit, Productivity is $6 per member per month, or $4.50 per member per month billed yearly ($54/year), and removes the 60-tab ceiling along with adding advanced search and duplicate removal. Team runs $10 per member per month, or $8 billed yearly, and adds SSO and centralized billing. Toby prices per seat because it's built as a collaboration tool, so those team-tier numbers scale with how many people share your collections, not with what one person alone would pay.

## If you're juggling several ongoing projects: Workona

Workona's framing is "Get your tabs under control," and it does that by grouping a project's tabs into a Space that auto-saves as you work and syncs across your devices. Close it down, pick it back up next week, and it's exactly how you left it. It's built for people running more than one active project at a time, each with its own cluster of tabs worth keeping organized rather than scattered across the tab bar.

The free plan is real, tucked into a pricing-page FAQ rather than shown as its own plan card, and capped at 5 spaces; Workona's own FAQ says it "can be used forever" below that cap, it just won't let you create a new space once you hit it. Pro is listed as "Starting at $7 / month," confirmed to be the annual-billed rate, and adds unlimited spaces, 90-day session backups, and integrations. Team starts at $8 per user per month with a 3-user minimum, again priced for groups rather than one person.

## If you want a tidy page of links and widgets: start.me

start.me calls itself, in its own title tag, a "Bookmark Manager, Custom Start Page & New Tab," and its hero line sums up the pitch: "All your important links, one click away." You build a page out of mostly first-party widgets, weather, RSS feeds, notes, bookmark groups, and click through to whatever those bookmarks point at. It's a genuinely mature product: a July 2026 changelog alone shipped a bookmark clean-up tool, a rebuilt Notes editor, new timer and page-introduction widgets, wider 7-column layouts, and AI-driven site suggestions.

The free plan costs nothing, includes advertising by start.me's own description, and caps you at three personal start pages, though widgets and bookmarks inside those three pages are unlimited. Personal PRO is $25 a year (about $2.08 a month), removes the ads, unlocks unlimited pages, and adds 27 PRO-only features and widgets. Team is $25 a month billed annually for 10 users, a different, group-priced product.

## If you want a handful of sites live and visible all day: TulipLot

This one's ours, so take the framing with that in mind: TulipLot doesn't save tabs, group them into projects, or build a page out of widgets. It renders. A TulipLot dashboard is a fixed 3×2 grid, six cells, and whatever sits in a cell is a live, interactive version of that actual site: an inbox showing new mail as it arrives, a calendar you click straight into, not a saved link or a bookmark pointing at either one.

Free accounts get five usable cells plus one ad cell fixed in the bottom-right slot; Premium drops the ad and unlocks all six for $4 a month. You can also try it with no account at all: two live cells load immediately at [/try](/try), saved to that browser only, and they carry into your dashboard automatically if you sign up later. Every cell fills from a built-in catalog or from any HTTPS URL you paste in yourself, and the free TulipLot Companion, a Chrome extension, unlocks embedding for many sites that would otherwise refuse to load in a frame. Not every site can be embedded this way, though: a handful of major login-gated services, Gmail and Google Calendar among them, never allow embedding under any circumstances and become one-click launchers instead, a cell you click to open the site in its own tab rather than seeing it live inline. If your problem is a small set of sites you check constantly rather than a growing pile of things to save, that's the job this fills.

## How to pick

If your problem is too many tabs piling up faster than you can use them, Toby solves that. If it's several ongoing projects each needing their own saved, restorable set of tabs, that's Workona's job. If you want a tidy home page of organized links, weather, and notes, start.me does that well and has for a while. And if the problem isn't saving or organizing anything, it's wanting a handful of specific sites open and live at the same time every day, that's what TulipLot is for. None of these are mutually exclusive; plenty of people reasonably run two at once, one for what they're not actively using and one for what they check constantly.

## Questions people ask

### What's the best free start page?

It depends on what you're trying to do, not which one wins outright. Toby's free plan gives you 60 saved tabs and unlimited members; Workona's free plan gives you 5 project spaces used forever below that cap; start.me's free plan gives you three ad-supported start pages with unlimited widgets and bookmarks; TulipLot's free plan gives you five live cells plus one ad. All four cost nothing to start, and the right one depends on which job matches your day.

### Do any of these work without creating an account?

TulipLot does: [the try page](/try) gives you two live cells with no account required, saved to that browser only, and it carries into your account automatically if you sign up later. That's the one entry on this list we can confirm doesn't need a signup, because it's ours and we control the page.

### Is a browser start page still worth using in 2026?

For plenty of people, yes, though not always in the portal-page sense the term used to mean. If your day is a handful of sites you check constantly, a dashboard or start page keeps them in one predictable place instead of buried in a pile of tabs. If it's genuinely just two or three sites, your browser's built-in start page or a couple of bookmarks already covers it, and none of the tools above are solving a real problem for you.

Whichever job matches your day, pick the tool built for it rather than whichever one ranks first on a list. If that's a handful of sites you want open and live at once, [try TulipLot](/try) with no account, or see exactly what [Free and Premium](/guides/premium-vs-free) each include before deciding.
