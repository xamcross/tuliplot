## Global Constraints

These bind every task. Task 1 is content maintenance; Tasks 2–5 publish claims about named companies, where the constraints are not style preferences but the difference between honest marketing and defamation.

**Competitor facts — the single source.** Every factual claim about Toby, Workona, or start.me must come from `docs/seo/2026-08-02-competitor-facts-verified.md` (verified 2026-08-02 from each vendor's own pages). If a fact you want is not in that file, either leave it out or re-verify it from the vendor's own site and add it to the file with its source. **Never source a competitor fact from an aggregator, listicle, or your own memory.**

**Do not publish — these are known-wrong and would be defamatory or actionable:**
- Toby's "up to 5 members per organization" cap. It survives only in a stale help article; Toby's current pricing page says "There are no more restrictions on how many members you have."
- "$6/month" for Workona Pro. The live page says **$7**; $6 is a stale aggregator figure.
- The "Workona cut its free tier from 10 spaces to 5" story as Workona's own statement. It is third-party reporting only — attribute it explicitly or omit it.

**The apples-to-oranges rule.** Toby and Workona *save references* to tabs you reopen later. start.me is a widget start page whose widgets are mostly first-party. **None of the three keep multiple live, interactive third-party apps rendered simultaneously** — which is TulipLot's entire product. A table row reading "5 cells vs 60 tabs vs 5 spaces" counts three unrelated things and misleads the reader. Compare *jobs to be done*, and state plainly where the products are not substitutes. A reader who buys TulipLot expecting a tab manager is a refund and a bad review.

**Pricing presentation.** Toby and Workona charge per seat; start.me and TulipLot do not. The fair single-user comparison is Toby Productivity $6/mo ($4.50 annual), Workona Pro $7/mo (annual-billed), start.me Personal PRO $25/year (≈$2.08/mo), TulipLot Premium $4/mo. Never blend team tiers into a single-user table. Only Toby prints "usd" explicitly — assume USD for a US reader but do not attribute the currency to Workona or start.me as their claim.

**Ad-supported free tiers.** start.me's free plan explicitly "Includes advertising," which is genuinely comparable to TulipLot's free tier. Toby and Workona do **not** describe their free tiers as ad-supported — do not imply symmetry.

**Tone.** Honest, specific, non-disparaging. These competitors are good products serving adjacent needs. Write the page you would be comfortable with their team reading. Say where they win — Toby for saving many tabs cheaply, Workona for project-based session management, start.me for a widget-rich start page — and where TulipLot's fixed live grid is the better fit. A comparison page that never concedes a point reads as marketing and converts worse than an honest one.

**TulipLot's own facts (verify against code, never assume):**
- Anonymous `/try`: **2 usable cells**, 3 signup-locked, 1 ad cell. Persists to `localStorage` in that browser only; carried into the account on signup. No account required.
- Free: **5 usable cells + 1 ad cell** (slot 5, bottom-right, fixed).
- Premium: **6 usable cells, no ad, $4/month.**
- Every cell control is identical across plans; Companion support is identical.
- Not every site embeds. `X-Frame-Options`/CSP block many; the Companion extension unlocks some; some (major-provider logins) never frame and fall back to open-in-tab. **Never imply everything embeds** — Wave 3 shipped that error and it had to be fixed.

**Content pipeline conventions:**
- Frontmatter keys: `title`, `slug`, `description`, `date`, `category`. `slug` must match the filename. `date` is `YYYY-MM-DD` (use `2026-08-02`).
- The body starts with a single `# H1` matching the title; `stripLeadingH1` removes it from the rendered prose.
- `### Question?` h3 sections are extracted into FAQPage JSON-LD. **Every FAQ answer must be true standing completely alone**, without the surrounding article — the FAQ is what Google and AI answer surfaces read. Wave 4 shipped an article whose body was corrected while its FAQ kept the wrong claim; do not repeat it.
- **Check `.tl-article` CSS before using a markdown construct you have not seen in these articles.** Wave 3 introduced the first `<table>` and `<h3>` into prose and neither had styling. Tables and h3 are now covered; anything else new (blockquote, nested list, image) needs verifying in `frontend/src/app/features/marketing/` styles first.
- Prose voice: **em-dash density under ~1.0 per 100 words.** Four existing guides sit at 1.8–2.1 and that is the clearest voice-drift signal in the corpus. Prefer commas, colons, and full stops.
